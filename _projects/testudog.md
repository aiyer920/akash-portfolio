## Overview

Testudog is a quadruped robot built to compete in the IEEE Robotic Quadruped Challenge. The quadruped has been developed from scratch, including the mechanical design, manufacturing, software and electronics. As the mechanical lead for this project, I contributed heavily to the design and manufacturing of the robot as well as to the development of testing devices for individual components. The project helped develop skills with CAD and design, manufacturing methods, and with leadership and mentoring other students.

## Key Features

The main components of the robot include the body, hips, and legs. The body houses the electronic components including various electronic boards and the battery. The body also provides routing for wiring and can act as a mounting surface for sensors. It is connected to each of the hips, which primarily route wiring from the legs to the body, connect both components, and hold the leg motors. The legs consist of the upper and lower legs. The lower leg has a foot at the end and is controlled by a belt at the knee which is linked to a motor in the upper leg. The upper leg is connected to two hip motors which rotate the upper leg forward/backward and outward/inward from the body.
## Design

The body was designed to house the electronics and route wiring. It features openings for wiring and a pegboard structure which allows electronic boards to easily snap on and off. This also gives the interior modularity for arranging the boards. Aluminum frames are at each end and center of the body to connect each half and make manufacturing easier by dividing the body into smaller sections. The body cover is removable and allows for easy access to internals which is important for fast troubleshooting which could be required at competitions. The hip was designed to mount to the body as well as the two hip motors, leaving room for the legs to rotate. The upper section of the leg runs from the motors to the knee, and houses the belt which rotates the lower leg at the knee. The lower leg starts at the knee, rotating due to the belt at the knee and has a circular rubber foot. Testudog had numerous requirements and complex designs, and the legs needed to be designed with heavy load and motor considerations, which taught me a lot about CAD and mechanical design in general.
## Manufacturing

High-stress sections like the upper legs and knees were manufactured out of aluminum. Another high-stress section was the lower leg, which is made from a carbon fiber tube. Covers for the upper legs and the body sections are made from PLA. The ends of the hip are made from aluminum plates, while the main center section of the hip was resin printed. The body frame pieces connecting the 3D printed sections were made from aluminum. The aluminum frame pieces, such as for the upper legs, body, and hips were manufactured using a waterjet, while more complicated aluminum pieces were made through CNC milling. Resin printing was used for thick, somewhat stressed sections like the hips, while more decorative and less load-bearing components like the body and leg covers were 3D printed with PLA. 3D printed parts needed to be manufactured in sections due to printer size constraints, which is especially relevant for the body sections. Manufacturing Testudog required a large range of advanced manufacturing techniques which greatly increased my knowledge of design for manufacturing and these techniques.
